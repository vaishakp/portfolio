abstract: In a binary black hole merger, the inspiral portion of the gravitational waveform
  corresponds to two distinct horizons orbiting each other, and the merger and ringdown signals
  correspond to the final horizon being formed and settling down to equilibrium. However, we
  still lack a detailed understanding of the relation between the horizon geometry in these
  three regimes and the observed waveform. In this thesis, we show that the well-known inspiral
  chirp waveform has a clear counterpart on black hole horizons, namely the shear of the outgoing
  null rays at the horizon. The shear behaves very much like a compact binary coalescence waveform
  with increasing frequency and amplitude, and the parameters of the system estimated from the
  horizon agree with those estimated from the waveform. We then track the source multipole moments
  of the dynamical horizons in simulations of several binary black hole mergers and calculate
  the corresponding Love numbers for axisymmetric modes. Unlike the (field) tidal Love numbers
  of non-spinning black holes, these do not vanish. We present evidence that, at least for modest
  mass ratios, their behaviour is universal. We extend this to the non-axisymmetric moments
  and show that the dynamical horizons encode detailed information about the dynamics of the
  binary, and that their source multipole moments are strongly correlated with the gravitational
  field at null infinity. Finally, we study the outer common dynamical horizon formed during
  the merger and its relationship with the outgoing gravitational waves, and show how the deformed
  common horizon approaches the final Kerr state by absorbing gravitational radiation with signatures
  of quasi-normal ringing. Its gravitational field shares a similar multipolar structure as
  that at future null infinity, and we point out some limitations, inconsistencies, and anomalies
  that need to be addressed.
